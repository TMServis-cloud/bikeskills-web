6. RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS ARISING FROM DEFECTIVE PERFORMANCE

Quality on receipt

If the goods received have shortcomings (e.g. they lack the agreed or legitimately expected properties, are unsuitable for their usual or agreed purpose, are incomplete, or their quantity, size, weight or quality does not meet other statutory, contractual or pre-contractual parameters), these are defects of the goods for which the seller is liable.

Within two years of receiving the goods at the latest, the buyer may claim from the seller, at their choice, free removal of the defect or a reasonable price reduction; unless this is disproportionate to the nature of the defect (in particular if the defect cannot be removed without undue delay), the buyer may request delivery of a new item without defects, or a new part without defects if the defect concerns only that part.

If repair or replacement of the goods is not possible, the buyer may request a full refund of the purchase price upon withdrawal from the contract.

If a defect appears within six months of receipt by a consumer buyer, the item is deemed to have been defective at the time of receipt.

The seller is not obliged to satisfy the buyer's claim if it proves that the buyer knew about the defect before receipt or caused it themselves.

For used goods, the seller is not liable for defects corresponding to the degree of previous use or wear. For goods sold at a lower price, the seller is not liable for the defect for which the lower price was agreed. Instead of the right to replacement, the buyer in these cases is entitled to a reasonable discount.

Statutory rights from defects

A consumer buyer is entitled to exercise rights from a defect that appears in consumer goods within twenty-four months of receipt

A business buyer is entitled to exercise rights from a defect that the item had when the risk of damage passed to the buyer, even if it appears later. The business buyer's rights also arise from a later defect caused by the seller's breach of its obligations.

The buyer is obliged to notify the seller of a defect without undue delay after they could have discovered it through timely inspection and sufficient care.

Within this period the buyer may file a complaint and, at their choice, request the following for a defect constituting a material breach of contract (regardless of whether the defect is removable or not):

  • removal of the defect by delivery of a new item without defects or delivery of the missing item;
  • free removal of the defect by repair;
  • a reasonable discount on the purchase price;
  • a refund of the purchase price upon withdrawal from the contract.

A breach of contract is material if the breaching party knew or must have known at the time of concluding the contract that the other party would not have concluded the contract had it foreseen the breach.

For a defect constituting a non-material breach of contract (regardless of whether the defect is removable or not), the buyer is entitled to removal of the defect or a reasonable discount on the purchase price.

If a removable defect recurs after repair (a third complaint for the same defect or a fourth for different defects), or the goods have a larger number of defects (at least three defects at the same time), the buyer may claim a discount on the purchase price, replacement of the goods, or withdraw from the contract.

The seller is not liable for defects caused by normal wear and tear or failure to follow the instructions for use.
